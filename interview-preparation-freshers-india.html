How Freshers Can Prepare for a Job Interview

Practical guidance for students, fresh graduates and job seekers · Updated November 2026

1. Read the job description carefully

Start by identifying the role's main responsibilities, required skills and eligibility criteria. Mark the skills you can demonstrate through coursework, projects, internships or previous work. Prepare one honest example for each important requirement. Do not claim experience simply because a phrase appears in the job description.

2. Research the employer

Read the organisation's official website, product or service pages and the role description. Understand what the company does, who its customers are and what the advertised position is expected to contribute. Check the interview invitation for the format, date, time, location or meeting link. Avoid relying on rumours or unverified interview-question lists.

3. Prepare a clear introduction

A useful introduction is brief and relevant. You can organise it in four parts: your education or current role, one or two relevant strengths, a project or experience that demonstrates them, and why the role interests you.

Example structure: “I recently completed my degree in [field]. During my studies, I worked on [project], where I used [tools or skills] to [specific contribution]. I am interested in this role because it would let me apply those skills while learning more about [relevant area].” Replace every bracket with true details and keep the answer natural.

4. Be ready to explain your projects

For each project, prepare to explain the problem, your individual contribution, tools used, decisions you made and what you learned. If it was a team project, clearly separate your work from the team's work. Be ready to discuss one challenge and how you approached it. Never invent results, users or performance improvements.

5. Practise common question themes

  • Tell me about yourself.
  • Why are you interested in this role?
  • Which project best demonstrates your skills?
  • Describe a problem you faced and how you handled it.
  • What are you currently learning?
  • What would you like to know about this role?

Practise speaking answers aloud, but do not memorise a long script. Specific examples are more useful than generic claims such as “I am hardworking” without evidence.

6. Prepare questions for the interviewer

Ask about the team's priorities, the work expected in the first few months, training for new employees or how performance is evaluated. Choose questions that are not already answered in the job description or company website.

7. Check the practical details

  • Confirm the interview time and time zone.
  • Test your internet, camera, microphone or travel route.
  • Keep a copy of your resume and relevant project notes ready.
  • Choose a quiet, suitable place for an online interview.
  • Join or arrive a little early.
  • Send a short thank-you message if appropriate.

After the interview

Write down the topics discussed and any follow-up action requested. If the employer gave a timeline, allow that period before following up. A polite message can thank the interviewer, mention the role and briefly reaffirm your interest. Do not send repeated messages in a short period.

Remember: Use these examples as starting points. Keep every statement accurate and adapt it to the role and employer's instructions.
