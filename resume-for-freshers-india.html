How to Create a Resume for Freshers in India

Your first resume does not need years of work experience to be useful. It needs to make your education, practical skills, projects and readiness for the role easy to understand. This guide explains how to prepare a clear, honest resume when you are applying for internships or your first job.

Quick principle: Keep the resume relevant to the role. Include real skills and work you can explain in an interview. Do not add tools, marks, certificates or experience you cannot verify.

1. Choose a simple resume format

For most fresh graduates, a clean one-page resume is a sensible starting point. Use a readable font, consistent heading styles, clear dates and enough white space. A two-page resume may be appropriate when you have substantial projects, internships, publications or relevant experience.

  • Use a familiar document layout with standard section headings.
  • Keep contact details near the top.
  • Use bullet points instead of long paragraphs.
  • Avoid decorative charts, excessive colours and unnecessary graphics.

2. Add the essential sections

  1. Name and contact details: Include your name, professional email, phone number, city and relevant portfolio or LinkedIn link. Avoid unnecessary personal details such as full residential address, date of birth or identity numbers.
  2. Professional summary or objective: Write two or three specific sentences about your qualification, strongest relevant skills and the type of entry-level role you are seeking.
  3. Education: List your degree, college or university, graduation year and marks or CGPA if they are relevant and accurately stated.
  4. Projects: Describe two or three meaningful academic, personal or team projects. State the problem, your contribution, tools used and the result.
  5. Skills: Group relevant technical and professional skills. Be prepared to demonstrate each technical skill you list.
  6. Internships, training and certifications: Add genuine experience with the organisation, dates and what you actually did. Distinguish training from employment.

3. Write a useful fresher resume summary

Avoid generic claims such as “hard-working and dynamic candidate seeking a challenging position.” Instead, mention your qualification, relevant area of interest and evidence of your skills.

Example for a computer science graduate

“Recent Computer Science graduate with hands-on academic project experience in web development and database fundamentals. Built a student project using HTML, CSS, JavaScript and SQL, and interested in an entry-level software development role where I can contribute while continuing to learn.”

Use this only as a structure. Replace the degree, technologies and project details with your own verified information.

4. Explain projects with evidence

Project descriptions are especially important when you do not yet have employment history. A useful bullet explains what you built, what you personally handled and which tools you used.

Weak: “Made a website as a college project.”

Clearer: “Developed a responsive department information website using HTML and CSS; created the navigation and course pages and tested the layout on mobile screens.”

Do not invent percentages, user counts or business impact. Add measurable results only when you recorded and can explain them.

5. Match the resume to the job description

Read the job description and identify the qualifications, tools and responsibilities that genuinely match your background. Use the employer's terminology naturally when it accurately describes your experience. Do not copy every keyword or claim skills you do not have.

  • Prioritise the most relevant projects and skills.
  • Use standard section names such as Education, Projects and Skills.
  • Spell out an acronym the first time if it may be unfamiliar.
  • Check that dates, job titles and qualification names are consistent.

6. Common fresher resume mistakes

  • Using the same generic resume for every role.
  • Listing too many unrelated skills without evidence.
  • Writing long paragraphs that hide your contribution.
  • Adding fake experience, certificates or project outcomes.
  • Including spelling errors, broken links or inconsistent dates.
  • Using a complicated layout that is difficult to read on screen.
  • Sharing sensitive personal information that an employer does not need.

7. Final checklist before applying

  • My name and contact details are correct.
  • My education details and dates are accurate.
  • Each listed project is real and I can explain my contribution.
  • The skills match the role and my actual ability.
  • The resume is easy to read on a phone and computer.
  • The exported PDF opens correctly and has a professional filename.
  • I have checked the employer's application instructions.
