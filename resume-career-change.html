How to Write a Resume for a Career Change

When moving into a different field, your previous job titles may not show the full value of your experience. A focused resume can help an employer see relevant responsibilities, transferable skills and recent preparation for the new role. The aim is to make the connection clear without overstating your background.

Start with the target role

Read several job descriptions for the type of role you want. Note the recurring tasks, tools and capabilities. Identify which ones you have used in previous work, volunteering, study or personal projects. Be realistic about gaps that still need training or practice.

Write a targeted summary

Use two or three sentences to explain your current background, the relevant strengths you bring and the direction of your transition. Avoid presenting yourself as already experienced in the new field if you are entering it for the first time.

Translate transferable skills

Examples of transferable skills include customer communication, scheduling, documentation, analysis, quality checking, team coordination and problem solving. Do not list them in isolation. Describe a real situation where you used them and connect it to the target role.

Reorder the resume around relevance

  • Place the most relevant summary and skills near the top.
  • Include projects, courses or volunteer work that demonstrate preparation for the new field.
  • Keep employment history accurate, with clear titles and dates.
  • Use accomplishment bullets that explain your contribution.
  • Reduce unrelated detail without hiding or falsifying your work history.

Show recent preparation

If you have completed relevant training, built a portfolio, volunteered or undertaken a genuine project, include it with accurate dates and descriptions. A course is not the same as professional experience; label it correctly.

Example of a transferable-skill bullet

“Coordinated weekly service schedules for a team of [number], tracked pending requests and prepared status updates for customers.”

Use this only if it describes work you actually performed. Replace the bracketed detail with a verifiable number or remove it.

Explain the transition

A cover letter can briefly explain why you are changing fields and how your previous experience supports the move. Keep the explanation positive and focused on the role rather than criticising a previous employer or industry.

Final checklist

  • Does the resume clearly identify the target role?
  • Are transferable skills supported by examples?
  • Are previous job titles and dates accurate?
  • Is recent training labelled correctly?
  • Have I avoided claiming experience I do not have?
