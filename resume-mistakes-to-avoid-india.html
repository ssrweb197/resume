12 Resume Mistakes Job Seekers Should Avoid

Practical guidance for students, fresh graduates and job seekers · Updated November 2026

1. Using one resume for every role

A general resume may miss the skills and evidence an employer is seeking. Read each job description and adjust the order of relevant skills, projects and experience. Tailor honestly; do not add skills you cannot explain.

2. Writing a vague objective

Statements such as “seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation” tell the reader very little. Use a short summary that identifies your field, relevant strengths and evidence. Freshers can mention a relevant academic project or practical skill.

3. Listing skills without evidence

A long list of tools does not show how well you can use them. Include skills relevant to the target role and support important ones through project descriptions, coursework, certifications or work examples. Be prepared to discuss each listed skill.

4. Describing projects too briefly

“Worked on a website” does not explain your contribution. State the project's purpose, your role, the tools used and what you completed. If the work was collaborative, identify your own contribution accurately.

5. Adding unsupported achievements

Do not invent percentages, revenue, rankings, users or awards. If you do not have a measurable result, describe a concrete deliverable or responsibility instead. Accuracy is more important than impressive-sounding numbers.

6. Including spelling and grammar errors

Proofread names, dates, email addresses, headings and bullet points. Read the document aloud or ask someone to review it. Spell-check tools can help but may not catch incorrect names or context.

7. Using a difficult layout

Decorative graphics, unusual fonts, dense tables and multiple columns can make information harder to scan or extract in some systems. A simple structure with clear headings is often a safer starting point, especially when an employer requests a particular format.

8. Making the resume too crowded

Prioritise information relevant to the role. Use concise bullet points and consistent spacing. There is no universal page limit for every profession and career stage; follow employer instructions and keep the document easy to read.

9. Forgetting dates and consistency

Use a consistent date format for education, employment and projects. Check that timelines are accurate and do not imply overlapping full-time roles if that is not what happened.

10. Using an unprofessional email address

Use an email address that is simple and appropriate for job applications. Check it carefully because a single typo can prevent an employer from contacting you.

11. Adding unnecessary personal details

Include contact and location information that is useful for the application. Avoid adding sensitive personal information unless the employer specifically and legitimately requires it. Follow local law and the employer's application process.

12. Sending the wrong file

Use the file type requested by the employer. Give the file a clear name, such as Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf, and open the final attachment before sending to make sure it is the correct, readable version.

Final review checklist

  • Target role and relevant evidence are clear.
  • All claims, dates and contact details are accurate.
  • Spelling, grammar and formatting are consistent.
  • Projects explain your own contribution.
  • The file opens correctly and follows application instructions.

Remember: Use these examples as starting points. Keep every statement accurate and adapt it to the role and employer's instructions.
