How to Write a Resume Summary: Examples for Freshers

A resume summary is a short introduction near the top of your resume. For a fresher, it should quickly tell a recruiter what you studied, which relevant abilities you have practised and what kind of entry-level opportunity you are seeking. It is not a place for unsupported claims or a long list of adjectives.

A simple three-part formula

  1. Background: Your degree, area of study or relevant training.
  2. Evidence: One or two skills supported by a project, internship or practical assignment.
  3. Direction: The role or work area you are applying for.

Usually two or three concise sentences are enough. Adapt the wording for each vacancy.

Example: engineering graduate

Recent [branch] engineering graduate with academic project experience in [relevant area]. Used [tools or methods] to complete [specific task] and document the results. Seeking an entry-level [role] opportunity to apply these skills and develop further.

Replace every bracket with accurate details. Do not claim professional experience if the work was an academic project.

Example: commerce graduate

Recent commerce graduate with coursework in accounting, business studies and spreadsheet-based analysis. Completed academic assignments involving [actual task or software]. Interested in an entry-level accounts or finance support role.

Example: arts or humanities graduate

Recent [subject] graduate with experience in research, written communication and preparing structured presentations through academic work. Interested in an entry-level role in [relevant field] where clear communication and careful research are useful.

Example: IT or computer science graduate

Computer Science graduate with project practice in [programming language] and [database or framework]. Developed [brief description of real project contribution] as part of academic work. Seeking an entry-level software or technical support role.

Common summary mistakes

  • Using phrases such as “highly dynamic” without evidence.
  • Writing a paragraph that repeats the entire resume.
  • Claiming expertise in tools you have only heard about.
  • Using the same summary for unrelated roles.
  • Adding personal details that are not relevant to the job.

Quick review checklist

  • Can a reader understand my background in a few seconds?
  • Does each skill match something I have actually done?
  • Is the target role clear?
  • Have I removed vague adjectives and repeated information?

Next step
