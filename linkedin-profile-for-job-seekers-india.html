How to Improve Your LinkedIn Profile for Job Applications

Practical guidance for students, fresh graduates and job seekers · Updated November 2026

1. Use a clear profile photo and background

If you choose to add a photo, use a recent, clear image that looks appropriate for your field. A simple background image is optional. Do not use someone else's photograph or misleading visual branding.

2. Write a specific headline

Your headline can identify your current status, target area and a few relevant skills. Avoid filling it with unrelated keywords or claiming a job title you do not hold.

Example for a fresher: “Computer Science Graduate | Java, SQL and Web Development Projects | Seeking Entry-Level Developer Roles”. Adjust the skills to match what you have genuinely practised.

3. Make the About section useful

Use a few short paragraphs to explain what you studied or do, the areas you are interested in, the skills you can demonstrate and the kind of opportunity you are exploring. Include a project example rather than relying only on adjectives. Keep personal contact details private unless you intentionally want them public.

4. Add projects with context

For each project, describe the problem, your role, tools and outcome. Link to a portfolio, code repository or demonstration only if it is yours to share and does not expose confidential information. Explain team contributions honestly.

5. Complete education and experience

Use accurate institution names, dates, qualifications and role descriptions. If you are a fresher, coursework, internships, volunteering and academic projects can help explain your preparation. Do not present coursework as paid employment.

6. Choose relevant skills

Prioritise skills connected to the roles you are applying for. Remove skills you cannot discuss confidently. Endorsements can add context, but they do not replace practical evidence.

7. Keep your profile consistent with your resume

Job titles, dates, education and project descriptions should not contradict your resume. Your LinkedIn profile can contain more detail, but the underlying facts should remain consistent. Update both when your circumstances change.

8. Make thoughtful connections

Connect with classmates, colleagues, alumni and people in relevant professional communities. When sending a request to someone you do not know, include a short, respectful note explaining the shared context or reason for connecting. Avoid mass messaging people with generic job requests.

9. Share useful professional activity

You can share a project milestone, a lesson from a course, a portfolio update or a thoughtful comment on an industry topic. Do not post confidential work, copied articles or claims that exaggerate your contribution. Consistency and authenticity matter more than posting every day.

10. Check privacy and job preferences

Review profile visibility, contact settings and job-seeking preferences. Decide which information should be public. Be cautious of messages asking for payment, passwords, one-time passcodes or sensitive identity documents before a legitimate hiring process.

Before applying

  • Headline clearly reflects your real skills and target area.
  • About section includes evidence, not only generic claims.
  • Projects and experience explain your contribution.
  • Dates and details match your resume.
  • Portfolio links work and contain no private information.
  • Privacy and contact settings are appropriate.

Remember: Use these examples as starting points. Keep every statement accurate and adapt it to the role and employer's instructions.
